Anatomy & credits

This educational game uses a simplified, rotatable 3D body. Paired organs and the intestines are grouped as single game pieces. Placements are approximate, with forgiving hit areas for small structures.

Original anatomy

The skeleton and nine organ models were supplied in e2e-draft1. They were rendered as transparent images, recolored, and positioned for this game.

  • BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Dataset.
  • Lung and liver surfaces: Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein, Human Reference Atlas / HuBMAP, 3D Reference Organ Set for Female v1.5 (2023), CC BY 4.0.
  • Original application: Human Atlas, MIT license.

Muscle surfaces

The ten active muscle groups use 32 BodyParts3D muscle surfaces from Human Atlas, in the same native coordinates as the skeleton. These surfaces were extracted, regrouped, recolored and given a subtle fiber texture; the tray images are renders of the same meshes. BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, CC BY 4.0. Simplified muscle groups show selected superficial and deeper structures; they do not represent every muscle.

Additional illustrations

Bladder, gallbladder, thyroid, trachea, esophagus, diaphragm, adrenal glands, eyes, tongue, appendix and thymus were generated with OpenAI image generation for this game. These are stylized teaching illustrations.

Anatomy references

General organ descriptions and relationships were checked against the National Cancer Institute’s SEER Anatomy & Physiology training, including thyroid anatomy and digestive system regions.

The palette is inspired by the supplied purple reference. This game is not an official AstraZeneca product.
